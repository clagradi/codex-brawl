# Frame data

Generated by `node tools/framedata.mjs` (do not edit by hand). Every number comes from simulating the game one tick at a time in headless Chrome; the game runs at 60 ticks per second.

How the numbers are measured

- Both players are the same fighter (a mirror match), the foe stands still, nothing else happens. Each move is started with the engine's own starter, and tick 0 is the press.
- **Startup**: the tick the hit lands (the press is tick 0; a startup of 6 means the hit lands on the 6th tick after the press). For shots, the tick the projectile appears.
- **Active**: how many ticks the hitbox can land (found by putting the foe back on its feet every tick). **Recovery**: the rest of the move (total minus startup minus active). **Total** is the length of the move until the fighter can act again.
- **Dmg** is the damage of the whole move against a foe who stands there (combo scaling included), **Chip** against a foe who holds the guard. **Hits** is how many hits landed.
- **On hit / On block**: frame advantage counted from the last contact of the move, after the hit stop: how many ticks sooner the attacker can act than the foe. Positive means the attacker acts first. Where two numbers are shown the first is for player 1 as attacker, the second for player 2 (see "Update order" below).
- **Reach** is the closest and the farthest distance in pixels between the fighters (centre to centre) at which the move connected, on a coarse ladder of distances (56, 68, 80, 94, 110, 128, 150, 180, 220, 270, 330, 410, 520, 680).
- The base game has no block stun: a guarding fighter can act again right after the hit stop, so every move is negative on block by its remaining recovery.

Update order: the game updates player 1 before player 2 inside one tick, so a foe that is hit by player 1 is one tick ahead in its stun compared with a foe hit by player 2. The tables show both numbers where they differ.
